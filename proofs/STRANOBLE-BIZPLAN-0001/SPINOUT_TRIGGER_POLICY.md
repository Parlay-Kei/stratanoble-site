# Strata Noble Consulting LLC
## Product Spinout Trigger Policy

**Policy Owner:** Stephen Hubbard, Founder & CEO
**Effective Date:** January 2026
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines when internal products developed within Strata Noble Consulting LLC become candidates for spinout into separate legal entities. It provides clear, measurable criteria to ensure spinout decisions are made consistently and at the optimal time for both the consulting business and the emerging product.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All software tools developed internally
- Productized service methodologies
- Platforms built for or derived from client engagements
- Any IP that could operate as a standalone business

This policy does **not** apply to:
- Client deliverables owned by clients
- Generic templates and documentation
- One-time tools with no commercial potential

---

## 3. Operating Principle

**Services fund products.**

Strata Noble Consulting LLC is a services-first entity. Revenue from consulting engagements funds internal R&D and product development. Products remain internal IP—and liabilities remain consolidated—until a spinout trigger is met.

---

## 4. Spinout Triggers

A product becomes a **mandatory spinout candidate** when **any one** of the following triggers is met:

### Trigger 1: Revenue Threshold

| Metric | Threshold | Measurement Period |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) | $5,000 | Sustained for 3+ consecutive months |

**Rationale:** $5K MRR demonstrates sufficient market validation and operational traction to justify the overhead of a separate entity. Three months of consistency filters out anomalies.

**Examples:**
- SaaS subscription product hitting $5K MRR
- White-label licensing generating $5K/month in fees

### Trigger 2: Investor Interest

| Metric | Threshold |
|--------|-----------|
| Formal investment interest | Term sheet or Letter of Intent (LOI) received |

**Rationale:** External investors require clean cap tables and dedicated entities. A formal term sheet signals serious intent and justifies the formation cost.

**Examples:**
- Angel investor presents LOI for $50K investment
- VC sends term sheet for seed round
- Strategic partner proposes equity investment

**Note:** Casual investor conversations or expressions of interest do NOT trigger spinout. Only formal written documents qualify.

### Trigger 3: Liability Exposure

| Risk Category | Trigger Condition |
|---------------|-------------------|
| **Payments Processing** | Product processes >$10K/month in transactions OR holds customer funds |
| **Scheduling at Scale** | Product manages appointments for >100 end-users/month |
| **Consumer PII at Volume** | Product stores PII for >500 unique consumers |
| **Regulatory Exposure** | Product enters regulated industry (healthcare, finance, etc.) |

**Rationale:** High-liability operations should be isolated to protect the core consulting business from catastrophic risk. Separate entities provide liability firewalls.

**Examples:**
- Booking platform processing deposits for barber shops
- CRM storing consumer contact information at scale
- Automation tool with access to client payment systems

---

## 5. Trigger Evaluation Process

### Step 1: Identification
Founder or operations lead identifies that a product may have reached a trigger threshold.

### Step 2: Documentation
Document the specific trigger met:
- Trigger type (Revenue / Investor / Liability)
- Supporting evidence (MRR reports, term sheet copy, user counts)
- Date threshold was reached

### Step 3: Decision
Founder reviews documentation and confirms spinout decision within 30 days of trigger identification.

### Step 4: Execution
If spinout is confirmed, initiate the Spinout Process (see separate IP Assignment & Licensing Outline).

---

## 6. Exceptions

The Founder may defer a spinout if:
- The product is being sunset or discontinued
- A merger or acquisition is imminent
- Temporary threshold breach (e.g., one-time revenue spike)

All exceptions must be documented with rationale.

---

## 7. Review Cycle

This policy will be reviewed:
- Annually (January of each year)
- Upon any trigger activation
- When new product categories emerge
